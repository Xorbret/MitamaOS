BABEL

בָּבֶל · Βαβέλ · バベル

When the tower fell, the builders scattered and their speech broke into a thousand tongues. In the aether the confusion never stopped, and every scattered tongue grew its own small, noisy spirits. Babel is what they're called together. It isn't an army. It's a rabble from every pantheon with no leader and no plan, gathering wherever the air is busy, because making a racket is fun.

Who they are

What they attack

Availability. Nothing is stolen. Things just stop working: the Wi-Fi drops, the network list fills with junk, the air goes dead.

Why

For fun. Babel's spirits are tricksters and pests. All they want from you is a reaction.

How they get on

Badly. They squabble in different languages and trip over each other. A Babel drill feels like a crowd, not a plot.

Where they fit

The first district. Babel is the noise you learn on before the factions with a real agenda arrive.

Babel's rules

Surge

The outage keeps running while you look. Every action point you spend on evidence also costs 5 uptime, because people are offline while you dig. Read one more log, or act now?

Fair by rule: no drill ships where reading everything and then playing the right Seal could still run uptime to zero.

Crosstalk

When two Babel spirits share a round, stopping the louder one first makes the quieter one's evidence free, because now you can finally hear it. Deal with the outage everyone notices, then the trick underneath.

No dice. The wrong order costs points but never locks you out.

Not every outage is Babel

Some drills are a harmless spirit getting blamed for Babel's noise. The right move is a Temizu fix, and burning a Seal on it costs points. Rule out the boring cause before you call it an attack.

In the Deep Aether: the Fallen Tower

Leave your Domain for the spirits' world and Babel's realm is the first layer down: the rubble of the Tower itself, descended floor by floor. Each floor is a mixed crowd. Surge doubles, and clues arrive "in another tongue", garbled, until Spectrum Eye is played. The Kamaitachi trio and Pan wait as elites. Each floor shows its spirits before you go down, and you can swap cards at the shrine between floors.
