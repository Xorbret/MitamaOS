THE GOOD NEIGHBOURS

Aos Sí · Tylwyth Teg · the Fair Folk · the Gentry

Nobody in Ireland, Scotland, Wales or Cornwall wanted to say their real name, so they called them the Good Neighbours and hoped the compliment kept them friendly. They live under the hills, wear glamours so nothing is what it seems, and they take: people, names, years. They have walked in through every door Babel left open.

Who they are

What they attack

Integrity. Nothing goes down. Everything looks fine, and that's the problem: the network, the login page and the "IT" calling you might all be copies.

Why

They take. A changeling left in the cradle, a traveller led off the road, a password, a session, a year of your life. Babel wanted a reaction. The Good Neighbours want something you own.

How they act

Courtly and patient. In the old stories they never tell a plain lie, they only let you believe one, and every gift has a price. One noble house, working together.

Where they fit

They follow Babel. The Kamaitachi trio's stolen handshake and the Gaki's starved address pool are exactly the openings they need. Babel breaks things. The Good Neighbours move in while they're broken.

Their rules

Glamour

One clue in a round may be glamoured: it looks exactly like the real thing and it's false. Spend 1 more point to verify a clue and the glamour falls away. Learning which clues to double-check is the skill.

Fair by rule: there's never more than one glamour per round, and the honest clues alone always point to the right Seal.

The Bargain

Once per drill a Neighbour offers a gift: 2 extra points now, or a free clue. Take it and the price comes due two rounds later, as a card taken from your hand or a slice of Purity. Refuse and you score the Unbound yaku.

Never needed. The real lesson: don't accept what you didn't ask for.

Cold iron

In every Celtic tradition, iron burns the fae. Any card that's Iron (Babel's Iron Road, and this faction's Iron in the Cradle) strips the glamour off one clue when you play it. Your Babel deck still matters here.

The first wave

Later waves

In the Deep Aether: Under the Hill

Through a door in the side of a fairy mound, into the hall where the music never stops. Time runs wrong down here: each floor is one round longer than it looks on the map. Shrines are glamoured too, so one of the three cards on offer at each shrine is a fae trick that turns to leaves when you play it, unless you verify it first. Never eat the food: the Bargain is offered on every floor. The Kelpie and Fear Dearg wait as elites.

Their cards

The GENTRY set. Seals carry the spirit they bind, and every support card is a real piece of fae protection from the folklore. Tap one for details.
