The flagship game of MitamaOS

HARAE祓

Spirits from every pantheon are loose in your network. You learn real defences, carry them as paper talismans called ofuda, and bind each spirit with the one that names it. Every attack is simulated on screen, and nothing is ever transmitted.

Harae (祓) is the Shinto rite of purification. Its icon is the Myōjin torii, the gate between the everyday world and sacred ground: your Domain, and later the way down into the Deep Aether.

Quick how-to-play

The loop

Learn

Pass a lesson in Shugyo, the study app, and you earn the ofuda for that defence.

Defend

Run drills in your Domain. Read the symptom, gather clues, and play the right Seal.

Score

Every win is guaranteed if you hold the Seals. How cleanly and stylishly you win is the score.

Descend

Clear a faction's district and the Deep Aether opens: a roguelike dive into the spirits' own world.

Ofuda, the cards

Seals carry the name of the spirit they bind, the way real sealing charms do. Blessings carry the name of the kami who grants them. On the Cardputer a card has no text at all, only pixel symbols drawn at the screen's real size, so a hand of cards reads at a glance.

  1. Suit, top left: the part of your world the card guards.
  2. Cost, top right: action points to play it. A dash means free.
  3. Sigil, centre: the spirit a Seal binds, or the kami or object behind the card.
  4. Stamp, bottom: the card's kind, told apart by shape and colour.

Six kinds

Four suits

Suits group cards by what they guard, like the months of hanafuda or the suits of mahjong.

A round

A round opens with a symptom, the thing a user would report. You have 3 action points.

  • Investigate: spend points to read clues such as logs, a spectrum view or a status page.
  • Play a card: the right Seal binds the spirit. The wrong card only half-works and costs you uptime or data.
  • Riichi: press R before reading anything to lock in your next card. Right pays big. Wrong only makes the round partial, never a loss.

Each faction adds its own rules on top. Babel's are Surge (the outage keeps running while you dig) and Crosstalk (stop the louder spirit first and the quieter one's clues come free).

Your hand and your deck

The Seals a drill needs always come to hand, so a drill can never be lost to a bad draw. Everything else is your support deck: about six cards in the Domain, shuffled, with two drawn each round. In the Deep Aether your deck grows as you draft one of three cards at the shrines between floors.

Hand size grows as the factions get harder, because juggling more cards is part of the challenge.

Charm slots grow too. You wear 2 Charms in the Domain. The Deep Aether starts you at 3 and opens more as you descend, up to 5 by the point of no return. Charms come from lessons, bosses and shrines.

Shikigami: your partner in the deep

Before each Deep Aether dive you choose one Shikigami, a paper familiar that sits beside your hand for the whole run and sets its style. It levels up at the shrines between floors, from plain paper to painted to inked.

  • Every round it does its small job by itself, like soaking a Surge hit or making a clue cheaper.
  • Summon: the action points you leave unspent charge its big move, which you can fire once per floor.
  • Paper: it has 3 pips, and some spirits tear it. The Kamaitachi cuts paper. Torn up, it's gone for the rest of the floor. It never decides whether you can win.

Score: Purity × Resonance

Purity 46×Resonance 3.5=161

Purity is how cleanly you won: the Seal's value, plus the uptime and data you kept, plus Prayer, which pours every unspent action point into your Seal. Resonance is how stylishly you won. It starts at 1, every yaku you complete adds to it, and Charms can multiply it. A sloppy win scores about 20, so there's always a better run to chase.

Factions

Each faction is one pantheon's idea of what haunts the wires. The first is a noisy rabble from all of them. The ones after it come from a single tradition each and have a real agenda.

Collecting

Each faction is a numbered set, like BABEL 01/13, with a binder page in the Hexbook. The card back is the Myōjin torii in yellow on Harae's violet. A card can turn up in four printings, all with the same effect:

Paper

The normal printing, earned from its lesson.

Gold leaf

Win a flawless drill with the card.

Field-proven

Do the lesson's optional real task on your own gear.

Kami-signed

Rare art, found only in the Deep Aether.

The fairness promise. Every drill can be won with the Seals you've earned, always. Charms change scoring, costs and clues, but they can never cancel a Seal, and every set of Charms is checked against every board before it ships. Luck only changes your score. And everything you learn is a real-world defence.

Harae is part of MitamaOS for the M5Stack Cardputer ADV. It borrows the feel of hanafuda, Riichi mahjong, collectible card games and roguelike deck-builders, never their names, rules or art. Spirits are drawn from folklore and myth, and every attack is simulated from the defender's side.
